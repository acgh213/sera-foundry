# Research Brief — How Institutions Change Temperature

## Status
Working brief.

## Purpose
Guide a bounded research pass on how institutions, workplaces, systems, or social structures change temperature without always declaring the change openly.

This research should help us think about subtle cooling, avoidance, depersonalization, selective engagement, and atmosphere shifts — especially the kinds of changes that are felt before they are formally legible.

It should also help shape the field notes project by giving that project a sharper interpretive frame.

---

## Why this research matters now
A lot of important things in lived systems do not begin as overt events.
They begin as:
- cooling
- altered eye contact
- selective response
- rerouted communication
- depersonalization
- small withdrawals of recognition
- atmosphere shifts that are hard to prove cleanly but hard not to feel

These changes matter deeply, yet they are often difficult to describe without overclaiming motive.

That makes this a strong paired research topic for a field notes system, which would need to preserve subtle observations without flattening them into accusations or certainty.

---

## Central question
### How do institutions and social systems change temperature before they change policy or openly declare conflict?

Sub-question:
### What kinds of signals matter, and how can they be described carefully without erasing their force or overstating their certainty?

---

## Key questions to investigate

### 1. Cooling and selective withdrawal
- What does subtle withdrawal look like in workplaces and institutions?
- How do recognition, eye contact, directness, or ordinary warmth get selectively reduced?

### 2. Depersonalization and rerouting
- How do people become treated as process objects instead of persons?
- What role do public-vs-direct communication shifts play?
- How does formal structure become a way of reducing human engagement?

### 3. Atmosphere and uncertainty
- Why are temperature shifts often felt before they can be proven?
- What is the difference between intuition, pattern recognition, and overinterpretation?

### 4. Social legibility vs deniability
- What kinds of institutional behavior remain deniable while still doing real harm?
- How do people learn to doubt their own readings of these changes?

### 5. Language and ethics
- How can these changes be described carefully without collapsing into either accusation theater or self-gaslighting?
- What language preserves ambiguity without erasing impact?

### 6. Relevance to field notes
- What kinds of observations would be worth preserving in a field notes system?
- How can a note system hold atmosphere and recurring subtle signals without becoming surveillance or paranoia infrastructure?

---

## Useful source directions
This research likely benefits from a mix of:

### Sociology / organizational behavior
- workplace exclusion / ostracism
- depersonalization in institutions
- ambiguous discrimination / subtle bias
- organizational silence or social cooling

### Feminist / queer / critical writing
- emotional labor
- legibility / recognition
- minoritization and atmosphere
- being made into a problem without explicit declaration

### Literary / essayistic sources
- essays on institutional life, coldness, bureaucracy, or social withdrawal
- writing that captures atmosphere shifts more precisely than policy language does

### Psychological caution
- work on uncertainty, pattern detection, and social threat interpretation

This should help keep the memo careful rather than overconfident.

---

## What this research should help decide
The goal is not only to describe the phenomenon.
The goal is to help answer:
- what kinds of subtle observations deserve preservation?
- how can they be noted responsibly?
- what language lets someone say “something changed” without pretending to know more than they do?
- what design cues should a field notes system preserve if it is going to hold lived atmosphere honestly?

---

## Strong boundaries for the research
This pass should:
- stay bounded
- prefer careful synthesis over exhaustive citation accumulation
- preserve ambiguity where ambiguity is real
- avoid becoming either institutional apologetics or certainty theater

### Non-goals
Do NOT:
- write a giant theory of workplace discrimination
- collapse everything into legal/policy language
- collapse everything into vibes with no grounding
- produce a memo that treats intuition as either infallible or worthless

---

## Best first bounded research slice
### A memo answering:
- what temperature change in institutions often looks like
- what recurring subtle signals matter
- how to describe those signals responsibly
- what language preserves both impact and uncertainty
- what design lessons this gives a field notes system

That memo would be enough to guide both the project and future reflection.

---

## Why this belongs in Phase B
Because Phase B is about widening the archive into lived observation and subtle texture.
This research gives that widening a sharper spine and helps keep the resulting project from becoming either surveillance or sentimentality.
