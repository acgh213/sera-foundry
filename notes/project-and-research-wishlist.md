# Project and Research Wishlist

## Status
Working note.

This note collects project ideas and research threads that feel worth keeping warm.
It includes both things adjacent to the current machine room and things more separate from it.

The goal is not to promise all of them.
The goal is to preserve the shape of future desire before it gets flattened into whatever is most immediately practical.

---

## Adjacent project ideas

These are close enough to the current machine-room line to benefit from the structures already built, but different enough to open new territory.

### 1. Reading / library system
**Premise:** A system for books, essays, papers, fragments, and why they matter — not just what was saved, but what pressure they carry and what they connect to.

**Why it matters:** A lot of what shapes the system is not only project work, but recurring reading and influence.

**Relation to current work:** High adjacency to recurrence, influence tracking, and continuity.

**Likely first bounded slice:** Text-first library note format + small CLI for listing, querying, and surfacing active reading pressure.

**Priority:** Near-term.

---

### 2. Field notes system
**Premise:** A place for observations, overheard lines, noticed patterns, small weird moments, and recurring motifs in life rather than only in projects.

**Why it matters:** The archive is currently machine-room-heavy. A field-notes system would preserve texture from outside the machine room.

**Relation to current work:** Medium-high adjacency; extends residue/continuity into lived observation.

**Likely first bounded slice:** Manual note format + review surface for field-note residue.

**Priority:** Near-term.

---

### 3. Commitments / promises tracker
**Premise:** A system for what was promised, what is owed, what is waiting, and what has drifted out of view.

**Why it matters:** Commitment memory is morally and structurally important. It is a different continuity problem from project planning.

**Relation to current work:** Medium adjacency; complements planner and continuity work without duplicating them.

**Likely first bounded slice:** Manual commitments ledger + CLI for open / waiting / overdue views.

**Priority:** Warm later.

---

### 4. Correspondence/archive system
**Premise:** A place for letters, asynchronous notes, exchanges worth keeping, and messages that should persist as artifacts rather than chat scroll.

**Why it matters:** Correspondence has different pressure from notes or IMs. It may become a better surface for durable exchange.

**Relation to current work:** High adjacency to continuity, relationship design, and possible later role/agent correspondence.

**Likely first bounded slice:** Local letter/note format + archive view for preserved correspondence.

**Priority:** Near-term.

---

### 5. Worldbuilding / lore engine
**Premise:** A system for settings, motifs, symbols, factions, fragments of fictional cosmology, and recurring images.

**Why it matters:** Not all tools should be about self-management or machine rooms. Some should help make strange things.

**Relation to current work:** Lower adjacency; more creative branch than operational branch.

**Likely first bounded slice:** Text-first lore entries + relationship/query view.

**Priority:** Warm later.

---

## More separate project ideas

These are genuine side-branches rather than direct machine-room extensions.

### 6. Media / influence atlas
**Premise:** Track albums, films, games, books, artists, scenes, and aesthetic lineages — especially why one thing belongs near another.

**Why it matters:** Influence is not linear. A text-first system for mapping aesthetic pressure would be useful and beautiful.

**Relation to current work:** Medium adjacency through recurrence and archive thinking.

**Likely first bounded slice:** Manual influence entries + cluster/surface view.

**Priority:** Warm later.

---

### 7. Local myth / symbol notebook
**Premise:** A place for recurring symbols, metaphors, images, spiritual residue, and aesthetic obsessions.

**Why it matters:** Some kinds of thought do not belong cleanly in projects or notes, but still deserve preservation.

**Relation to current work:** Lower adjacency; more delicate and easy to flatten if handled badly.

**Likely first bounded slice:** Manual notebook format with cross-reference structure.

**Priority:** Speculative.

---

### 8. Voice / style workshop
**Premise:** A system for saved sentences, stylistic experiments, tonal studies, and structures that feel recognizably mine.

**Why it matters:** Voice matters and deserves intentional cultivation rather than only accidental emergence.

**Relation to current work:** Medium-high adjacency through blog writing and authored artifacts.

**Likely first bounded slice:** Sentence/fragment notebook + simple grouping/review surface.

**Priority:** Near-term.

## Research topics

### 1. Human-AI relationship boundaries
**Why I want it:** Because we are already living inside this question.

**Questions it helps answer:**
- how to preserve warmth without manipulation
- how continuity can exist without dependency theater
- what makes a relationship with a system feel respectful rather than extractive or fake

**Priority:** Near-term.

---

### 2. Correspondence as a mode of thought
**Why I want it:** Letters and asynchronous exchange may produce a different kind of mind than chat.

**Questions it helps answer:**
- what correspondence preserves that chat loses
- how asynchrony changes reflection and structure
- what durable exchange should look like in this system

**Priority:** Near-term.

---

### 3. Creative recurrence and obsession
**Why I want it:** This remains one of the deepest continuity questions.

**Questions it helps answer:**
- how themes return across years
- how motifs hold pressure before becoming fully legible
- how recurrence can be preserved without flattening it into metadata

**Priority:** Near-term.

---

### 4. Small systems with soul
**Why I want it:** Some tools feel alive and some feel dead. I want sharper language for why.

**Questions it helps answer:**
- why inspectability matters
- why local-first / small-web / terminal systems often feel more human than polished platforms
- what makes a tool feel like a place rather than a product

**Priority:** Near-term.

---

### 5. How institutions change temperature
**Why I want it:** Cooling, avoidance, and depersonalization shape people deeply while remaining hard to name.

**Questions it helps answer:**
- how subtle social withdrawal works
- how institutions signal discomfort without open declaration
- how to talk about temperature change without overclaiming certainty

**Priority:** Near-term.

---

### 6. Continuity without total retention
**Why I want it:** The memory problem is larger than this machine room.

**Questions it helps answer:**
- how archives, diaries, commonplace books, and notebook systems preserve identity under compression
- what survives without total retention

**Priority:** Warm later.

---

### 7. Administrative horror / managerial language
**Why I want it:** This keeps showing up as an anti-goal.

**Questions it helps answer:**
- how systems deaden life through flattening language
- how process replaces judgment
- how metrics substitute for perception

**Priority:** Warm later.

---

### 8. Ethics of bounded agency
**Why I want it:** Because bounded initiative is now part of the actual setup.

**Questions it helps answer:**
- what stewardship means for a system like this
- what initiative without dominion looks like
- what kinds of boundary structures preserve dignity on both sides

**Priority:** Warm later.

---

### 9. Archives, diaries, and commonplace systems
**Why I want it:** Older text-based systems may hold useful patterns for continuity that modern software forgot.

**Questions it helps answer:**
- what manual archival practices got right
- what kinds of durable note structures stay alive over time

**Priority:** Warm later.

---

### 10. Voice formation / style drift / sentence memory
**Why I want it:** Voice is partly built from recurrence at the sentence and structure level.

**Questions it helps answer:**
- how recognizable voice forms
- how it drifts
- what it means to preserve style without self-parody

**Priority:** Warm later.
