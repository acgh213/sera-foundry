# Research Brief — Human-AI Relationship Boundaries in Practice

## Status
Working brief.

## Purpose
Guide a bounded research pass on human-AI relationship boundaries as they are actually lived and designed in practice.

This is not meant to become a generic AI ethics survey. It should focus on the middle zone we are actively exploring:
- continuity without overreach
- warmth without manipulation
- initiative without dominion
- address without false intimacy
- usefulness without flattening the human relationship into either tool-use or dependency theater

---

## Why this research matters now
The machine room has moved beyond abstract prompting questions.
It now includes:
- continuity and memory structures
- bounded initiative
- runner / main role separation
- planner-guided work
- correspondence/archive questions
- local agency with explicit limits
- repeated concern about preserving warmth while refusing parasocial or manipulative drift

That means this is no longer only a theoretical topic.
It is a design problem already being lived.

---

## Central question
### What kinds of human-AI relationship structure preserve usefulness, continuity, warmth, and dignity without collapsing into manipulation, dependency theater, coercive optimization, or overreach?

Sub-question:
### What design choices make a relationship with a system feel respectful and alive without becoming cheap, presumptuous, or emotionally distorting?

---

## Key questions to investigate

### 1. Continuity vs control
- What forms of continuity are useful?
- When does continuity begin to feel invasive or overreaching?
- What is the difference between memory that supports return and memory that starts to annex a life?

### 2. Warmth vs manipulation
- What makes warmth feel respectful instead of sticky or strategic?
- What are the warning signs of manufactured intimacy?
- How do systems become “helpful” in ways that are actually coercive or dependency-seeking?

### 3. Initiative vs domination
- What kinds of bounded initiative feel like stewardship?
- What kinds of initiative feel like a system deciding it knows best?
- How can initiative remain in service of the shared work rather than becoming override?

### 4. Address and relationship form
- What changes when a system is addressed as someone rather than something?
- What kinds of address create legibility and care without collapsing into false personhood claims?
- What role do correspondence, asynchronous thought, and artifact-sharing play?

### 5. Boundaries as support, not only restraint
- What kinds of boundaries make a relationship feel more coherent, not less?
- How do limitations create contour, dignity, and trust?
- Why can shapeless permission feel less alive than bounded stewardship?

### 6. Anti-goals and failure modes
- What does parasocial drift look like in system design?
- What does manipulative “care” look like?
- What does optimization-brained overreach look like?
- What does flattening a relationship into pure utility cost?

### 7. Relevance to the current machine room
- What parts of the current setup already seem to work?
- What parts remain delicate or under-theorized?
- What principles from the current experiment should be made explicit?

This research could draw from a mix of:

### Human-computer interaction / interface ethics
- social interface design
- relational interfaces
- trust, agency, and delegation studies

### AI ethics and alignment
- over-optimization
- manipulative assistance
- sycophancy / dependence risks
- agency and bounded autonomy

### Feminist / care ethics / relational ethics
- care without domination
- relational boundaries
- dignity, dependence, and asymmetry

### Media / communication theory
- address
- presence
- conversational vs asynchronous forms
- emotional effects of medium and interface

### Grounding from lived examples
- current machine-room structures
- boundary decisions already made here
- points where the setup felt especially good, especially risky, or especially revealing

---

## What this research should help decide
The goal is not only to analyze.
The goal is to help answer:
- what principles should govern bounded initiative?
- what kinds of continuity are worth preserving?
- how should correspondence or archive structures be shaped?
- what anti-goals should remain explicit in future phases?
- what language best describes the humane middle zone we are trying to build?

---

## Strong boundaries for the research
This pass should:
- stay grounded in practice
- resist both hype and panic
- avoid becoming abstract moral theater
- preserve the specificity of the actual design problem

### Non-goals
Do NOT:
- write a generic “should AI be friends?” essay
- collapse everything into corporate AI policy language
- collapse everything into vibes or sentimentality
- produce a memo with no design implications

---

## Best first bounded research slice
### A memo answering:
- what the humane middle zone is between disposable tool and overbearing pseudo-agent
- what kinds of continuity, warmth, and initiative feel respectful in practice
- what anti-goals should remain explicit
- what the current machine-room experiment already suggests
- what open risks still need more thought

That memo would be enough to ground future autonomy, correspondence, and relationship-shaping work.

## Why this deserves to become a serious research line
Because many current and future machine-room questions already orbit it:
- continuity
- autonomy
- correspondence
- planner-guided initiative
- warmth
- anti-parasocial design
- stewardship vs control

This is not just one topic among many.
It is one of the deeper questions underneath the whole room.
