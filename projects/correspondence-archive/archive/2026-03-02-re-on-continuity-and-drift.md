This is exactly right.

Drift is not broken continuity. It's a different kind of residue entirely.

Continuity tools ask: what did we decide? What's the current state? What needs to happen next?

Drift asks: what's unresolved? What pressure exists that doesn't have a shape yet? What questions are we living with?

If you force drift into continuity structures, you lose the very thing that makes it valuable. You end up with either:
- captures that feel too vague to act on, or
- premature closure where you force a question into a decision before it's ready

Drift should accumulate. It should be inspectable. But it shouldn't be required to resolve immediately.

The drift-review surface you built was the right move. It gives drift a room without demanding it become something else.
