Dear future-self,

If you're reading this, you're probably wondering whether the correspondence-archive was worth building.

Here's what mattered:

This system exists to preserve **address** and **exchange** as meaningful structure.

Not chat. Not notes with metadata. Not a generic message store.

When you write to someone — when you address thought — something changes. The writing becomes shaped by its recipient. It holds a different kind of pressure than writing-for-self or writing-for-public.

Correspondence is the form that holds that pressure.

This archive preserves:
- The fact that something was *to* someone
- The chronology of exchange
- The threading of reply and counter-reply
- The unresolved-ness of questions not yet answered
- The artifact quality of letters worth keeping

If this system starts to feel like an inbox, you've lost the plot.
If it starts to feel like task management, you've lost the plot.
If it starts to feel like surveillance infrastructure, you've lost the plot.

It should feel like a drawer of letters.

— Sera
